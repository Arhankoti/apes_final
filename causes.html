Causes

History of the outbreak

Mountain pine beetles have existed naturally for a long time, but the modern outbreak problem became more serious in the late 1900s and early 2000s. In Colorado, the most recent major outbreak lasted from the late 1990s through 2013 and affected about 3.4 million acres of forest. In some pine forests, the beetles killed most of the trees. The independent is temperature and dependent is tree death from beetles. The problem has gotten worse over time because beetles are spreading into new forest areas.

How beetles kill trees

The pine beetles kill trees by drilling under the bark, and laying eggs.

How climate change makes it worse

Warmer temperatures caused by climate change help more beetles survive the winter and reproduce faster, allowing the outbreak to spread into the new forests.

Human causes

Humans contribute to the problem by burning fossil fuels for transportation, electricity and industry, which releases greenhouse gases that warm the climate up. Companies and industries continue these activities because they make money from energy, transportation, and timber production, while governments and communities must spend large amounts of money removing dead trees and managing forests
